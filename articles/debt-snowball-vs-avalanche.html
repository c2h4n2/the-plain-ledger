Debt Snowball vs. Avalanche

Both methods do the same basic thing: pay minimums on everything, then throw every spare dollar at one target debt until it's gone, then roll that payment onto the next one. The only difference is which debt you pick as the target.

The avalanche method

Target the debt with the highest interest rate first, regardless of balance. Mathematically, this is always the cheapest way to get out of debt — you pay the least total interest over the life of the payoff.

The snowball method

Target the debt with the smallest balance first, regardless of interest rate. It usually costs somewhat more in total interest, but it produces a fully paid-off account faster, which is where its real advantage lies.

Why the "worse" method often wins in practice

Debt payoff is as much a behavioral problem as a math problem. The snowball method's early wins — closing an account, seeing a debt hit zero — build momentum that keeps people going for the months or years a full payoff plan requires. The avalanche method is cheaper on paper but can feel like slow progress for a long time if the highest-interest debt also happens to be the largest balance.

A rough way to decide

  • Choose avalanche if: the interest rate gap between debts is large (for example, a 24% card versus a 6% loan), or you're confident you'll stay motivated without early wins
  • Choose snowball if: you've started and abandoned a payoff plan before, or the encouragement of quick wins matters more to you than shaving some interest cost

A middle option

Some people use a hybrid: knock out one or two very small balances first for quick momentum, then switch to attacking whichever remaining debt carries the highest rate. It captures some of the snowball's early motivation without giving up as much to interest.

The best payoff method is the one you'll still be following in month eight.

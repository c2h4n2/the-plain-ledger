Sinking Funds: The Budgeting Trick for Irregular Expenses

Car registration, holiday gifts, an annual insurance premium, a friend's destination wedding — none of these are emergencies, but they're also not monthly expenses, which means they don't fit neatly into a typical budget. A sinking fund is simply a savings bucket for an expense you know is coming, saved for gradually before the bill arrives.

How it's different from an emergency fund

An emergency fund covers the unexpected — job loss, medical bills, urgent repairs. A sinking fund covers the expected but irregular — expenses you can see coming on a calendar, even if the exact amount isn't certain yet. Mixing the two tends to leave your emergency fund constantly raided for predictable costs it was never meant to absorb.

Setting one up

  1. List the irregular expenses you already know about — car registration, an annual subscription, holiday spending, a friend's wedding, home maintenance
  2. Estimate the cost and the date it's due — even a rough guess is far better than no plan at all
  3. Divide the cost by the number of months until it's due — that's your monthly sinking fund contribution
  4. Automate a small transfer for each fund, the same way you would for any other savings goal

A simple example

If holiday spending typically runs about $600 and there are 10 months until the holidays, setting aside $60 a month means the money is simply there when the bills come due — no scrambling, no credit card balance carried into January.

Keep the buckets separate, even if the account isn't

You don't need a separate bank account for every single sinking fund — a shared spreadsheet or a note tracking "Car: $340 of $500 saved" alongside a single savings account works fine, as long as you know how much of the total balance is earmarked for each purpose.

A sinking fund turns a predictable expense into a non-event. The bill still arrives, but the stress doesn't.

Where this fits with everything else

Sinking funds usually sit inside the "savings" portion of a monthly budget, alongside your emergency fund contribution and any extra debt payoff. If you're not sure how much room is left for sinking funds after covering essentials, the budgeting basics guide walks through the split.

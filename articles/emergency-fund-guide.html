How Big Should Your Emergency Fund Be?

"Three to six months of expenses" is the answer you'll see everywhere, and it's not wrong — but it's also not specific enough to be useful. The right number depends on how stable your income is and how quickly you could replace it if it disappeared.

Start with expenses, not income

Base the fund on your essential monthly expenses — housing, utilities, food, insurance, minimum debt payments — not your full income. If a job loss happened tomorrow, discretionary spending would be the first thing cut anyway, so there's little point building a cushion around it.

Adjust the multiple to your situation

  • 1–2 months: dual-income household, stable jobs, low fixed costs, some family backup available
  • 3–4 months: the standard starting point for most single-income or moderately stable households
  • 6–12 months: freelance or commission-based income, a single-income household with dependents, or a specialized job that would take longer to replace

Build it in two stages

Trying to save six months of expenses before doing anything else can feel so far away that people give up. A more workable order:

  1. Stage one: save a starter fund of $1,000–$2,000 — enough to absorb a car repair or medical bill without going to a credit card
  2. Stage two: after high-interest debt is under control, build the rest of the full emergency fund alongside continued debt payoff

This is why most structured payoff plans put a small starter fund before debt payoff, then pause full emergency-fund building until high-interest debt is handled — see the comparison of debt snowball vs. avalanche for how that trade-off usually gets sequenced.

Where to keep it

A high-yield savings account, separate from your everyday checking account, is the usual answer — accessible within a day or two, but not sitting in the account you spend from daily where it's easy to dip into without noticing.

The goal isn't to earn the best possible return on this money. The goal is that it's there, untouched, when something goes wrong.

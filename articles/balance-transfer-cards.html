When to Use a Balance Transfer Card (and When It's a Trap)

A balance transfer card moves existing debt onto a new card, usually one offering 0% interest for a promotional period — often 12 to 21 months. Used well, it can meaningfully cut what you pay in interest. Used carelessly, it can leave you worse off than before.

When it genuinely helps

  • You have a clear payoff plan for the promotional period. If you can realistically pay off the transferred balance before the 0% window ends, most or all of the interest you'd otherwise pay simply disappears.
  • You understand the transfer fee. Most cards charge 3-5% of the transferred amount upfront. On a $5,000 balance, that's $150-250 — factor this into whether the move actually saves money.
  • Your current rate is high enough that the math clearly works. Moving a balance from 24% APR to 0% for 18 months is usually a clear win, even after the fee.

Where it commonly goes wrong

  • The old card stays open and gets used again. Without a plan to avoid this, some people end up carrying two balances instead of one — the transferred debt plus new spending on the original card.
  • The promotional rate ends before the balance is paid off. Whatever's left typically jumps to a standard APR, sometimes higher than the original card's rate.
  • A missed payment can void the promotional rate early. Many issuers' terms allow this — a single late payment can trigger the standard APR retroactively or immediately.
  • A hard credit inquiry and a new account can temporarily affect your credit score — usually minor and short-lived, but worth knowing about if you have a major loan application coming up soon.

A rough way to decide

Balance transfers tend to work best as a tool inside an existing payoff plan, not as a replacement for one. If you don't yet have a fixed monthly payment and a target payoff date, a transfer risks just delaying the interest rather than reducing it — the underlying spending or payoff habits need to be in place first.

A balance transfer buys you time at a lower cost. It doesn't pay the debt off by itself — you still have to do that part.

Compare the numbers

Before transferring, use the debt payoff calculator to compare your current payoff timeline against a 0% scenario minus the transfer fee, so the decision is based on your actual numbers rather than the promotional headline rate.
